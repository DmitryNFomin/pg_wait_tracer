## What exists
- The feature: when the tracer starts, PostgreSQL backends are already running. "Discovery admission" attaches to them and tries to PROVE, per backend, from which instant its numbers are exact, so that the startup gap in coverage can be closed and windows after it certified complete.
- Its contract: contracts/merge-v1/CONTRACT.md section 6 on origin/master (DECISIONs 30-45) with 190 oracle rows (contracts/merge-v1/oracle-discovery/ROWS.md, EXPECTED.md) after nine amendments in five days; the amendment records are compare-delta*.md beside them.
- Its implementation, none of it on master, all switched off: the merge side (branch agent/988-discovery-merge, 13 rounds; src/core/merge.c, src/merge_capture.c), the producer (branch agent/988-discovery-producer; src/discovery_producer.c, kernel programs in src/bpf/), a coordinator (branch agent/988-discovery-coordinator; src/discovery_integration.c), an independent exhaustive enumeration test (branch agent/988-merge-enum, 2.35 million schedules). Reviews: scratchpad/a2go/disc/review-merge-*.md, scratchpad/a2go/integ/review-*.md. The interface specification: scratchpad/a2go/integ/spec-frozen.md.
- Known limits already written down: certification is latched off for the whole capture by the first fault; recovery after a fault (REARM) is not a production path; a failed retraction write can leave a false "complete"; on PostgreSQL 13 three helper processes are handled by a separate route; the continuity witness does not exist.
- What the product does WITHOUT this feature: read contracts/merge-v1/CONTRACT.md sections 3-5 and DECISIONs 25, 25b, 25c, the coverage contract contracts/coverage-a2-v1/CONTRACT.md, and docs/REWORK_PLAN_2026-11-11.md, and say it exactly (what a user sees for a backend that was already running when the tracer started: its first interval, when its numbers become certified, what windows read as).
- The owner's standing goals: every number shown is correct; the UI works without glitches; overhead first, then correctness, then a realistic workload; EL8 4.18 and PostgreSQL 13 must work; for anything the tracer cannot do: "say it clearly in the interface and documentation BUT application cant fail or raise exception".

## Questions
1. **What user-visible outcome does discovery admission add** over the product without it, stated as what a DBA sees and for how long after tracer start (minutes? until every pre-existing backend exits? forever for pooled connections?). How often does that situation occur in real deployments (tracer restarted while a connection pool holds long-lived backends).
2. **Is the current design near the smallest that delivers that outcome correctly, or is it oversized?** Name the parts that carry most of the 190 rows and the review findings (the three admission routes; the quiet-baseline bracket; revocation of committed proofs; the forward-only latch; terminal dispositions; numeric-only EXITs; the PostgreSQL 13 no-wait-word route), and for each say whether it is essential to the outcome or exists to cover a corner that a simpler rule would make impossible.
3. **Give the two or three materially simpler designs** that still never show a wrong number (fail closed where they cannot prove), each in five lines: the rule, what the user sees, what it deletes from the contract and the code (rows, DECISIONs, files), what it loses against the current design, and what of the work already done it reuses. Candidates to consider, and better ones if you see them: (a) no proof at all for pre-existing backends: each is uncertified until it exits or is observed from a fresh start, the interface says "N backends were already running; their numbers are estimates until they reconnect"; (b) one route only (the first observed transition after attach is the bound; no quiet baseline, no no-wait-word route), everything else uncertified; (c) certify per window instead of per capture (a window is complete only if every backend active in it was observed from its start or from a bound).
4. **Is the process the problem?** Every undecided corner has been closed by adding a pinned row and a contract sentence (nine amendments). Is that the right response, or is the number of corners itself the signal that the state space should have been cut by design? What should the lead have done differently on 20 November when this was approved, and what should the lead do today.
5. **Recommendation:** continue the current design to completion (say what remains and why it will converge), or switch to one of the simpler designs (say what it costs to switch now and what it saves), or a hybrid (land the simple one first, keep the proof machinery as a later improvement). One recommendation, with the reason the owner would care about.
