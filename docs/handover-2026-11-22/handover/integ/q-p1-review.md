## Questions
1. **The continuity input has no producer fact behind it (review item 8).** After a REARM the merge needs, per lifetime with a recorded ADMIT, independently qualified evidence that the ORIGINAL startup attachment continued through the REARM into the current epoch; without it that lifetime's first batch faults. The review finds no fact in the frozen header that lets a coordinator set it truthfully for a numerical attachment, and for the no-wait-word route only a POINTER snapshot that P1 emits when the observation changes. What is the smallest producer fact that is actually TRUE evidence of that continuity on each route (what the kernel or the producer can observe: the watchpoint still installed on the same task and address since enable with no detach, re-arm or loss counted; the pointer observation uninterrupted), who stamps it and when (at REARM request? at each refresh?), and where it goes: a header delta to the frozen section A (give the member and its three states), a translation rule in section B, or a different shape for the merge input. Which round carries it (P1 fix round, P2), and what must the coordinator do when the fact is unknown (the contract says: fault at first batch).
2. **Blocker B2 and the review's question: which reconciliation cut the CENSUS record carries when the fence fails once and is proved at a later refresh.** Decide the rule (the record is re-issued with the later cut and ordinal; or the first census is permanently pending and a new census record supersedes it; or a failed fence makes the capture's census unknown for good), consistent with sections A and C (the coordinator's census re-read of cursor 0, immutable old cuts in P2's checks).
3. **Blocker B1:** a failed-open PID later held by a fork-born or non-member process is now emitted as an INVENTORY record while the census says inventory=0. Confirm the rule (CONTRACT.md around line 316: such holders are not inventory; only the number is resolved) and say what record, if any, the producer emits for that holder.
4. **Should-fix S2:** a failed pointer-watch ENABLE is recorded as a known continuity break; the merge treats breaks as permanent, so one failed enable blocks the no-wait-word route for that lifetime for good. Is "known break" the right fact for an enable that never succeeded (as against unknown continuity, or attempts-ended), given section A's three-valued rule?
5. **Should-fix S3 (loss of pointer observation):** a fire the observer missed on a lifetime that exits before the next snapshot leaves neither a break nor an evidence-total increment, and a sticky fault stops the comparisons altogether. Is there an observable that closes it at exit (the perf count read at teardown compared with fires observed), or is it a stated limit; and what should a sticky fault do to the evidence totals.
6. **Should-fix S1** (after stop_attempts, attach returns -EPERM even for fork-born and uninventoried backends; backend.c only falls through on -ENOENT): confirm it belongs in the P1 fix round and not P2.
7. **Order:** one P1 fix round (B1, B2, S1-S3 as decided) after the qualification round now running, then P2; or fold the continuity fact and the census rule into a short specification delta first. Does the coordinator round (I2) wait for any of it.
